Override vs Migiri

Choose Override if your main gap is a private, physical interruption after an urge starts. Consider Migiri if you want a broader system built around continuous adult-content blocking, daily recovery material, progress features, optional accountability, and access to additional support.

Product information checked November 22, 2026 · Features and pricing can change

Fast answer

Override is an urge interruption app with temporary shielding. Migiri is a broader blocker-and-recovery platform. The better fit depends on whether your priority is the acute moment, ongoing filtering and curriculum, or both.

The main difference is the job each product leads with

Override leads with the moment the urge is already active. You tap Break the Urge, selected apps are temporarily shielded, and an active real-world reset creates friction between impulse and action. It requires no account or accountability partner.

Migiri leads with ongoing protection and a wider recovery program. Its public product information describes adult-site and app blocking, daily exercises, progress and awards, an optional Companion accountability feature, an AI recovery coach, and optional one-to-one sessions.

Where Migiri may be the better fit

Migiri may suit you if you want a single product to cover more of the recovery process: ongoing filtering, structured educational material, progress tracking, optional accountability, and access to broader support. It may also be more appropriate if your first requirement is blocking adult websites across browsers rather than temporarily shielding a selected set of apps.

Where Override may be the better fit

Override may suit you if you already understand your pattern but lose access to your plan once the urge starts. Its narrow focus is the intervention window: reduce negotiation, add physical friction, and create a context shift. It may also fit buyers who do not want an account, community, recovery coach, or accountability partner.

Can Override and Migiri work together?

Potentially. Continuous filtering can reduce easy access before an urge, while an active interruption can give you something to do after the urge is present. If you combine tools, check for conflicts between system permissions, VPN-based filtering, and app-shielding controls on your device. More layers are useful only if the setup remains understandable and reliable.

What to verify before choosing

  • Which devices and browsers need protection?
  • Do you want continuous filtering, a moment-of-urge response, or both?
  • Do you want another person involved?
  • Are you comfortable with the account, VPN, and data permissions required by the features you plan to use?
  • What are the current trial, subscription, and cancellation terms?
